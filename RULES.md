== Stats ==
# Body
Everything related to your physical self
- this is your main stat if you want to play a…
    >fighter, thief, dancer, athlete, race car driver, fighter pilot, soldier, ballerina
- it is used to perform actions such as…
    >fighting, climbing, running, balancing, picking a pocket, operating a vehicle, acting gracefully, resisting a physical condition such as disease or hunger.

# Mind
Everything related to your mental self
- this is your main stat if you want to play a…
    >magic user, mechanic, hacker, mentalist, scientist, psychic, strategist
- it is used to perform actions such as…
    >casting a spell, repairing, hacking, research, strategic thinking, reading a mind, resisting mind control, not being afraid, functioning under stress, staying calm

# Social
Everything related to interacting with others
- this is your main stat if you want to play a…
    >negotiator, interviewer, politician, musician, cheerleader, entertainer, influencer
- it is used to perform actions such as…
    >negotiating, gathering information, lying, influencing, entertaining

## Losing stats
Sometimes a character might lose stats. This could occur for any number of reasons, for example stepping on a trap, crashing a car, failing to cast a spell, embarrassing yourself in front of a royal. There are two time frames for stat loss: scenic and temporal.

### Scenic
Some scenes during gameplay might impose momentary stat losses to characters, for example telling off color joke could reduce character’s Social stat, but it would be immediately recovered once the scene is over as the characters move to another location or talk with someone else (who didn’t hear the joke); or loud music in a club could be distracting a hacker imposing a Mind penalty for them until the music is turned off or character leaves the club.

### Temporal
If a character suffers harm that usually means the lose appropriate stat, for example falling off a roof would lower their Body. A ghost possessing someone would lower their Mind. Crawling through sewer would lower their Social.

## Recovering stats

Body and Mind recover 1 point per hour of in game time and cannot be recovered higher than the character’s starting stat. First aid and therapy can speed up recovery at the tables discretion.

Social recovery usually requires an action to performed such as showering or dressing up and isn’t usually time based.

Any stat lowering below -5 takes the character out of the scene. Body:-6 means the character is unconscious. Mind:-6 means the character is out of their minds and effectively a panicking NPC. Social:-6 means the character is too embarrassed to function.

Note to GM! There is no mechanical killed state, no matter how far below -6 character’s Body goes they will wake up after they have recovered back to Body:-5, unless the table decides it is appropriate narratively for the character to die, for example going below Body:-5 in a street corner probably doesn’t kill you, but you might wake up without wallet and shoes, however going below Body:-5 in a cannibal cave with multiple cannibals still functioning probably means the character won’t wake up.

# Character creation

assign -1, 0, and 1 and to any stats.

== Skills ==
Skills are freely chosen by players, but with the agreement of the table. Skills should inform about your character’s past and be specific. For example “combat” is very broad skill and as such not suitable for most tables, but “expert sniper” fills same kind of niche, but has specific context where it is useful.

All characters start with 2 skills.

== Checks ==
Checks are by default are 2d6+stat vs DR7 (Difficult Rating), players roll all checks and are trying to meet or beat the DR so DR7 means combined results of 7 or above is a success. Any result below DR is a failure.

Which stat to use is determined by the action performed, use your imagination or refer to stats section for inspiration.

Note to GM! Players shall always know the DR of the action they are attempting, this is so they can engage if they want to use a tool or a skill (or possibly even push their skill) before they act.

If a character has an skill that is appropriate to help with the check, for example “ballet dancing” when performing a Body based dancing check they can upgrade a die to a higher die (d6->d8).
If a character wishes they can push their skill to its limits, marking it until end of the session and increasing the upgrade by a die size, for example turning a d6 into d10. A marked skill cannot be used to gain die upgrades.

If a character has an item that is appropriate to help with the check, for example a Star Map when performing a Mind based strategy check they can upgrade a die to a higher die (d6->d8). Some items can be extra useful and upgrade your die twice, for example a legendary demon slaying sword should turn your d6 into a d10 instead.

Die progression
d6->d8->d10->d12->d20

Note! If a character has both an appropriate skill and item they can choose to apply die upgrades to separate or the same die, meaning d6->d8 twice or d6->d8->d10. Or if they are pushing their skill and using extra useful item they can choose to roll 2d10 or d6+d20.

Note to GM! The game starts with 2d6+stat, but with any skill you are probably looking mostly at d8+d6+stat. d8+d6 average around 8 with starting stats arranging from -1 to 1. This means a character rolling a check with their main stat, using a skill will average 9. Items with die upgrade should also be easy to obtain so average is 10.

Critical Success
If both dice roll their maximum number the roll is considered a Critical Success. This means that the check passes regardless of the actual end result and DR and usually it means that the check succeeds even better than originally expected or to the maximum degree. For example hacking a computer system with critical success could mean the system already had a known back door and the files the character was looking for are well named and indexed while with normal check additional hacking checks could be needed to find what they were looking for.
Note to GM! Only let players roll for things that are plausible since Critical Success is a thing. If there is no way to steal a cup from the vampire lord’s hand without them noticing don’t let player attempt such a thing, just tell them that such action will be noticed, but let them do it if they choose to suffer the consequences.

Fumble
Rolling two 1s on any set of dice regardless of end result and DR is a fumble, this means not only did the character fail, but they failed in the worse possible way. When a character fumbles they can choose from the following:
- Lose a stat point from the stat they were using.
OR
- If a skill was used and isn’t marked they can mark it.
OR
- If an item was used and it isn’t broken it can become broken, lost, or run out of fuel whatever feels appropriate for the table.
Note to GM! Additionally there should be narrative consequences, however try to avoid killing characters based on a single fumble.

== Rules of play ==
GM should go around the table in clock wise (or down the list in a VoIP) and ask every player in turn what they would like to do. Every player describes an action they wish to perform, maybe roll a check if the outcome is uncertain and then the spotlight moves to the next person. Once everyone has had a chance to do (or not do) something it is time for GMs upkeep. During upkeep GM advances any timers by one and narrates what is happening around the players. Note to GM! Do not re-describe what the players did, let players describe it themselves, do not repeat information. Tell players what they missed. For example: while you were fighting the space pirates the space military bursts in yelling “everyone hands up!”.

Rule of cool. Play the game however you like, but the system is much more fun if you are relaxed about it. Do things that are cool. Do things that aren’t safe. It is a game after all and death is always optional. Nothing sucks the fun as fast as a trying to be extra careful and checking every corner for traps and security cameras and sleeping trolls. Just head in and have fun. Trust your GM to keep the game going and even if you make a mistake character creation is fast and you can be slipped back in at the very next scene. This is also where the table needs to suspend their disbelief a little, introducing a new character so everyone can play is more important than patching plot holes.



== Optional rules section ==
— Luck —
stat that is used for rolls that don’t fit under any other stat. This isn’t a main stat and is rolled with a d3-1, so your luck starts between 0 and 2. Luck does not regenerate during a session, but is instead rerolled at the start of each session regardless of what value it has.
- it is used to perform actions such as…
>searching for treasure, seeing if door was left unlocked, games of chance

Additionally Luck can be spent on lowering DR by 2 or re-rolling a die (your’s or someone else’s). Luck can’t go below 0 from spending it, but can go to negatives due to Fumbles. At Luck:-6 you will automatically Fumble every luck roll regardless of the actual result.

— Violence —
Sometimes fleeing from a fight isn’t enough and characters need to inflict violence on NPCs.

Fighting should always be done with Body stat. Player chooses who their character is going to fight and rolls a Body/Fighting check using appropriate skills and equipment.

- Critical Success
>character inflict weapon damage + 1 damage to the NPC
- Success
>character inflict weapon damage to the NPC
- Failure
>both the character and the NPC inflicts weapon damage to each other
- Fumble
>on top of normal Fumble consequence the NPC also inflicts their weapon damage to the character

# Fighting DR
Base DR for fighting without any obstacles is 7, but environmental obstacles, such as trying to shoot something through smoke should increase it.

Also number of enemies increases the base DR. For every enemy within range where they can reasonably affect the character imposes +1 on the DR. For example a knight fighting three enemies means fighting check of base DR10, even if one or more of the enemies are not within “melee range” as long as they are equipped with ranged weapons (or ample amounts of rocks). Regardless of number of enemies players will make one fighting check on their turn.

For players to engage the threat of the NPCs GM shall describe their Type when introducing hostile NPCs into a scene or when asked by the player, this information shall not be hidden, but only Enemy Type should be revealed not HP nor any extra abilities.

# Enemy Types

---
1. Grunt
standard enemy players face early and often
HP: 1-4
Damage: 1
DR: base
Example: goblin, wolf, laborer, police officer, cultist, face hugger
---
2. Elite
harder enemies that function as early bosses and become standard later
HP: 3-6
Damage: 2
DR: base+1
Example: orc, bear, high security guard, swat, cult leader, xenomorph
---
3. Boss
bosses should be at end of archs which involve violence prominently
HP: 4-8
Damage: 3
DR: base+2
Example: war boss, dragon, military cyborg, demon, alien queen
---
4. God
characters are not expected to be able to defeat gods in combat, unless that is the vibe you are going for
HP: 10+
Damage: 4
DR: base+3
Example: god of thunder, eldrich being, ruler of the underworld, space king
---

Note to GM! To spice up encounters you can introduce extra abilities at their discretion to any Enemy Type. Also just because example contains something that doesn’t mean it has to be there. In some games players might be regularly fighting demons so it makes more sense to make them Type:1 Grunt. In another table Type 3:Boss Goblin makes more sense. These are guidelines for players to engage the threat of the situation so they can consider if they are equipped and skilled enough to take on the enemies.

# Weapon Types

---
0. Unarmed:
Players will always inflict 1 damage regardless of what they fight with as long as it makes narrative sense, for example kicking a car won’t damage the car in a meaningful way.
---
1. Common weapon
whatever is a common weapon in the universe of your table.
Damage: 1
Upgrade die by: 1
Example: dagger, pistol, brass knuckles, baton, stick
---
2. Uncommon weapon
whatever is more lethal and less common in the universe of your table
Damage: 2
Upgrade die by: 1
Example: sword, spear, battle axe, rifle, pistol, knife, blaster
---
3. Rare weapon
whatever is rare in the universe of your table
Damage: 2
Upgrade die by: 2
Example: magic sword, machine gun, shotgun, cannon, grenade
---
4. Special weapons
anything that does not fit the previous sections.
Damage: 3+
Upgrade die by: 2+
Example: legendary demon killing blade, antimatter pistol, rocket launcher, nuclear bomb, space laser
---

Note to GM! Enemies use their damage number regardless of what they are equipped with. Their weapons only affect narratively. For example it is okay for a Type1:Grunt enemy to shoot down player’s helicopter with a rocket launcher, but that shouldn’t deal more than 1 damage to player’s character in that moment.

— Getting Better —
During a longer campaign style play players may want to upgrade their character you can do so by…

Choosing between:
- New skill
OR
- Choosing a stat and rolling a d6, if the result is higher than the stat increment that stat by one

Characters should Get Better at the end of a story arch regardless of whether they achieved their goals or not.
Note to GM! This is up to you to decide, sometimes it could be fun to Get Better just before a boss fight for example.
